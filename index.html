TL;DR

OpenThermalAction is a thermal human action recognition dataset of 2,488 videos (981,879 frames) from 55 subjects performing 28 single-person and two-person actions, with 17-joint skeleton annotations for every person in every frame. Skeleton-based models trained on it reach up to 98.57% Top-1 accuracy on an unseen environment while running in 5–25 ms per clip on a single GPU, and the best model, MS-G3D with 3.0M parameters, exceeds the Top-1 accuracy of UniFormerV2 with 353.7M parameters on the same recordings.

Abstract

Human action recognition (HAR) has achieved remarkable progress with the advent of deep learning and large-scale RGB datasets. However, RGB-based approaches often degrade under challenging illumination conditions, motivating the use of thermal sensing as a complementary modality. Despite its potential, research on thermal HAR has been constrained by the lack of large-scale datasets with high-quality pose annotations. To address this gap, we present OpenThermalAction, the largest thermal HAR dataset with frame-level skeleton annotations. The dataset comprises 2,488 thermal videos from 67 subjects performing 28 action classes, including both single-person activities and multi-person interactions, resulting in approximately 982K annotated frames. We validate the annotation quality against manually annotated ground truth, achieving a mean OKS of 0.863, a PCK@0.05 of 96.4%, and a mean PJPE of 6.00 pixels. Furthermore, we establish a comprehensive benchmark using graph-, transformer-, and state-space-based action recognition models under cross-subject and cross-environment evaluation protocols. The results demonstrate strong generalization across unseen subjects and environments, with the best-performing model achieving top-1 accuracies of 98.57% and 95.80% for single-person and multi-person actions, respectively. The dataset, source code, and pre-trained models are available on the project website to bolster research in this area.

Contributions

  • OpenThermalAction dataset. 2,488 thermal videos and 981,879 frames of 55 subjects performing 28 action classes (15 single-person sports exercises and 13 two-person interactions), recorded at 640×480 and 30 FPS in three indoor environments. It is the largest publicly available thermal action dataset with skeleton annotations.
  • Frame-level skeleton annotations. 17-joint skeletons for every person in every frame, generated with a thermal-domain pose estimator and corrected by human moderators. Against manual labels on held-out frames, the released annotations reach an AP of 90.8, a PCK@0.1 of 99.0% and a mean joint error of 6.0 px, with no false positives.
  • Cross-environment benchmark. Seven skeleton-based models (five GCNs, a transformer and a state-space model) trained on the balcony recordings from scratch and with fine-tuning from NTU RGB+D 120, and evaluated on the unseen office and in-the-wild recordings with 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of each video.
  • Comparison with video-based HAR. Accuracy, parameter count and single-clip inference time of the skeleton-based models against TSM, VideoMamba and UniFormerV2 trained on the same thermal recordings. The dataset, source code and pre-trained models will be publicly released.

Videos were recorded with a 640×480 thermal camera at 30 FPS in three indoor environments: an open balcony area, an office, and unconstrained in-the-wild settings (an atrium, corridors and a conference hall). The first session covers 15 sports exercises performed by each subject from a frontal and a side view; the second covers 12 predefined two-person interactions and three unconstrained ones.

Skeletons were generated with a YOLO11x-pose model fine-tuned on thermal images from the same camera, keeping the most confident detection per person, and were subsequently inspected and corrected by human moderators. On 447 held-out office frames with 579 manually annotated persons, the released annotations reach an AP of 90.8 (AP50 98.0), a PCK@0.1 of 99.0%, a mean joint error of 6.0 px (1.5% of person height), and no false positives.

Results

Seven skeleton-based models (ST-GCN, AA-GCN, CTR-GCN, ST-GCN++, MS-G3D, SkateFormer, SkeletonMamba) were trained on the balcony recordings, both from scratch and fine-tuned from NTU RGB+D 120 weights, and evaluated on the unseen office and in-the-wild recordings. Test videos were additionally truncated to their first 25%, 50% and 75% to assess early action recognition. Fine-tuned MS-G3D reaches 98.57% Top-1 on single-person and 95.80% on multi-person actions of the office set with full-length input. The same recordings were used to compare against video-based models.

Ethics statement

The data collection protocol was approved by the institutional research ethics committee of the authors' institution and conducted in accordance with established guidelines for research involving human subjects. All participants provided informed consent for the use of the recordings in research and in the development of machine-learning models. No personally identifiable information was collected or stored; the released skeletons consist of joint coordinates only.

This page accompanies an anonymous submission under double-blind review. Author names, affiliations, and repository links are withheld until the review process is complete.

Model names refer to the original publications of ST-GCN, AA-GCN, CTR-GCN, ST-GCN++, MS-G3D, SkateFormer, SkeletonMamba, TSM, VideoMamba and UniFormerV2. NTU RGB+D 120 weights were used for fine-tuning.
